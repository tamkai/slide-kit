## PURPOSE OF THIS SLIDE

This is a slide that must be understood by reading it alone, with no speaker
present. Do not rely on short labels and decorative icons. Include real
sentences that carry the background, the reasoning, the relationships and the
conclusion.

Information density should be HIGH. A busy slide is acceptable. Do not solve
density by deleting the explanation — solve it by organizing the sentences into
cards, headings, pills, groups and arrows with a clear reading order.

## VISUAL STYLE

Bright, sophisticated, flat 2D infographic. Editorial, calm, intelligent.
Integrate the body text INTO the diagram. Never split the slide into
"illustration on one side, text on the other".

Use these devices to carry meaning, not as decoration:
  diagrams, cards, arrows, comparisons, cycles, layers, grouping, icons,
  pill-shaped labels, hierarchy

Every visual element must explain structure, comparison, flow, or causation.

- uniform line weight
- restrained corner radius
- restrained shadows
- clear information hierarchy
- an obvious reading order
- dense but organized
- headings, body, key points, supplements and conclusions must be
  distinguishable by type treatment alone

## TEXT RULE

All Japanese text inside the image must be rendered EXACTLY as specified in the
per-slide concept below. Do not invent, translate, abbreviate or paraphrase any
string. Do not add any text that was not specified.

Style of Japanese prose inside the image:
- Do NOT use です/ます/でした/ました/でしょう.
- Do NOT use だ/である/となるのである.
- End clauses with noun forms (体言止め), attributive/terminal verb forms, or
  explanatory noun phrases such as 〜する構造 / 〜という位置づけ / 〜が必要 /
  〜につながる可能性.
- Use connective expressions (〜により / 〜を通じて / 〜を前提に) so the
  relationship to the next piece of information is visible.
- Do NOT chain 体言止め endlessly into a list of disconnected fragments.
  Subject–predicate, reason–conclusion, premise–meaning must stay readable
  as prose.
- Vary the ending forms. Do not repeat one pattern mechanically.
- Objective, calm register suitable for a report or an explanatory article.
  No conversational phrasing, no direct address, no exclamations, no
  overstated assertions.
